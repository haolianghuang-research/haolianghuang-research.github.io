Estimating hard-to-measure vehicle dynamics

Vehicle signals provide an indirect view of the interaction between the tires and the road. My internship explored how CAN-bus time series could support estimation of tire–road friction and other vehicle-dynamics parameters.

The work combined foundation-model architecture design, initial experiments on small-scale datasets and preparation of larger CAN-bus datasets for subsequent training.

Model design and prototyping

I designed and investigated time-series model architectures for the estimation task, including Transformer-based temporal modeling and prediction of related vehicle-dynamics quantities. Small-scale experiments supported iteration on the model structure and training setup.

The broader direction was to learn useful representations from vehicle signals and support multiple estimation tasks, with friction estimation serving as a central application.

Data preparation and signal analysis

I prepared CAN-bus recordings for model training and evaluation, including preprocessing, normalization, temporal alignment, label-quality checks and diagnostic analysis of signal behavior.

Recording-level data splits kept overlapping windows from the same drive together. I also worked on signal-quality and leakage checks so that model comparisons reflected meaningful estimation behavior.

Research and engineering contributions

  • Connected model design with data preparation and repeatable experiment workflows.
  • Built diagnostic tools to inspect signals, model outputs and estimation errors.
  • Prepared large-scale CAN-bus data to support subsequent foundation-model training.
